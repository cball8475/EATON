STOP / DO NOT ENTER — restricted area signage

Three sheets, each 7 × 10 in (a standard OSHA sign size that prints on letter stock). Built to ANSI Z535.2-2011, which OSHA accepts as an alternative to the legacy design under 29 CFR 1910.145(f)(1)(ii). Print at 100% / “actual size” — not fit to page, which shrinks the color areas and letter heights below spec.

Pick the right signal word

  • Sheets 1 and 2 — DANGER. Only where entry exposes a person to an immediate hazard that will cause death or serious injury: energized equipment, an automated cell with no interlock, a robot envelope, a confined space, a live press brake.
  • Sheet 3 — NOTICE. Where entry is restricted by rule but there is no immediate injury hazard: secured stock, records or server room, gate-controlled yard. Posting DANGER on a low-hazard door is the fastest way to train people to ignore red.

Colors (ANSI Z535.1)

#C8102E safety red (PMS 186C)   #005EB8 safety blue (PMS 300C)   #000000 black   #FFFFFF white

Legibility

At 7 × 10 in the signal word cap height is ~0.80 in and “DO NOT ENTER” ~0.54 in, which reads at roughly 25–30 ft. If it has to be read from farther, print larger — change --w / --h and the @page size at the top of the stylesheet (10 × 14 → ~40 ft, 14 × 20 → ~55 ft).

Before posting

  • Fill in the hazard on the blank line. A sign that names the hazard gets obeyed; a generic one gets papered over.
  • Mount centered 5–6 ft above the walking surface, at the point where the decision to enter is made — not past it.
  • Laminate or mount on rigid stock. 1910.145(d) requires signs to be durable and free of sharp edges, burrs, and splinters. Paper taped to a door is not a sign.
  • If it is guarding a hazardous energy state, it supplements LOTO (1910.147) and machine guarding (1910.212) — it never substitutes for either.
  • Log the location and add it to the compliance calendar for periodic condition checks.
