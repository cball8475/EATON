# Gap Assessment — Take This Cold

45 items, 5 per domain. **Do not study first.** The point is a baseline, and a baseline you
warmed up for tells you nothing.

Rules: closed book, 45 minutes, calculator allowed on the math items, write your answers on
paper before checking. Short-answer format on purpose — recognition is easier than recall,
and the exam will make you recall.

Scoring is at the bottom.

---

## Domain 1 — Mathematical Calculations

1. What number is the base for OSHA incidence rates, and what does it represent?
2. A site worked 620,000 hours and had 9 recordable cases, 4 of which involved days away and
   2 restricted duty. Calculate TRIR and DART.
3. What is the OSHA permissible exposure duration for 100 dBA, and what exchange rate did
   you use?
4. Type B soil, trench 10 ft deep, 3 ft wide at the bottom. What is the width at the top?
5. Convert 35 ppm of a substance with MW = 78 to mg/m³.

## Domain 2 — Safety Programs and Concepts

6. List the six levels of the ANSI/ASSP Z10.0 hierarchy of controls, in order.
7. Is fault tree analysis inductive or deductive? Which direction does it run?
8. Give the three defining criteria of a confined space and the four criteria that make it
   permit-required.
9. In what order do you test the atmosphere of a confined space, and why that order?
10. State the six steps of the LOTO application sequence.

## Domain 3 — Ergonomics

11. Which OSHA standard covers ergonomics? (Careful.)
12. Name the four primary ergonomic risk factors.
13. Which nerve is compressed in carpal tunnel syndrome, and which fingers are affected?
14. Which assessment tool would you use for a nurse repositioning a patient, and why not
    RULA?
15. For a fixed-height workbench used by many operators, should you design for the 5th
    percentile, the 95th percentile, or something else?

## Domain 4 — Fire Prevention and Protection

16. Name the four legs of the fire tetrahedron.
17. What flash point divides flammable from combustible liquids under NFPA 30?
18. What class of fire is a magnesium chip fire, and what agent do you use?
19. What is the maximum travel distance to a Class A extinguisher? A Class B?
20. What does a vapor density greater than 1.0 mean for hazard control?

## Domain 5 — Emergency Preparedness and Response

21. List the three response priorities in order.
22. Under 1910.38, when may an emergency action plan be communicated orally?
23. What is the difference between HAZWOPER "operations" level and "technician" level?
24. What is the difference between unity of command and unified command?
25. What is the ANSI Z358.1 requirement for eyewash location, flush duration, and water
    temperature?

## Domain 6 — Industrial Hygiene and Occupational Health

26. Which of these is legally enforceable: PEL, TLV, REL?
27. Define STEL, including how often it may be reached and the interval between exposures.
28. What is the aerodynamic cut point of the respirable fraction, and where does it deposit?
29. What is the APF of a full-facepiece air-purifying respirator? Of a pressure-demand SCBA?
30. Define a Standard Threshold Shift.

## Domain 7 — Environmental Management

31. What is the difference in scope between RCRA and CERCLA?
32. Give the three RCRA generator categories with their monthly generation thresholds.
33. What is the pH criterion for a corrosive hazardous waste?
34. What are the due dates for Tier II reporting and TRI Form R?
35. In ISO 14001, what is the difference between an aspect and an impact?

## Domain 8 — Training, Education, and Communication

36. Name Kirkpatrick's four levels in order.
37. Which Kirkpatrick level does a post-class satisfaction survey measure, and what does it
    predict?
38. Give the two-part OSHA definition of a competent person.
39. Name the four stages of the DuPont Bradley Curve in order.
40. In Reason's taxonomy, what is the difference between a slip and a mistake?

## Domain 9 — Legal

41. Name the four elements OSHA must prove for a General Duty Clause citation.
42. How long does an employer have to report a fatality? An amputation?
43. How many days does an employer have to contest a citation, and what happens if they
    don't?
44. Name the four employer categories under the multi-employer citation policy.
45. How long must employee exposure records be retained? Medical records?

# Answer key

1. **200,000** — 100 full-time employees working 40 hr/wk for 50 wk/yr.
2. TRIR = `(9 × 200,000)/620,000` = **2.90**. DART = `(6 × 200,000)/620,000` = **1.94**
   (4 days-away + 2 restricted).
3. `T = 8/2^((100−90)/5)` = `8/2²` = **2 hours**, using OSHA's **5 dB** exchange rate.
4. Type B = 1:1 → 10 ft of run each side → `3 + 10 + 10` = **23 ft**.
5. `(35 × 78)/24.45` = **111.7 mg/m³**.
6. Elimination · substitution · engineering controls · **warnings** · administrative
   controls · PPE.
7. **Deductive, top-down** — start at a defined top event and work backward to basic causes.
8. Confined space: large enough to enter and work · limited or restricted entry/exit · not
   designed for continuous occupancy. Permit-required adds any of: hazardous atmosphere ·
   engulfment potential · internal configuration that could trap or asphyxiate · any other
   recognized serious hazard.
9. **Oxygen → flammable → toxic.** Combustible-gas sensors require oxygen to read correctly,
   so an oxygen-deficient atmosphere invalidates the LEL reading.
10. Prepare for shutdown · notify affected employees · shut down normally · isolate energy
    sources · apply lockout/tagout devices · release stored energy and **verify** isolation.
11. **There isn't one.** The 2000 standard was repealed under the Congressional Review Act
    in 2001; ergonomic hazards are cited under the **General Duty Clause**.
12. Force · repetition · awkward/static posture · duration. (Contact stress, vibration, and
    cold are the secondary stressors.)
13. **Median nerve**; thumb, index, middle, and the thumb-side half of the ring finger.
14. **REBA** — whole body including legs, and it handles dynamic, unpredictable postures.
    RULA covers only the upper limbs.
15. **Neither — design for adjustability.** If truly fixed, the strategy depends on whether
    the dimension is clearance (large user) or reach (small user).
16. Fuel · oxygen · heat · **uninhibited chemical chain reaction**.
17. **100 °F.** Class I (flammable) below; Class II and III (combustible) at or above.
18. **Class D**; **dry powder** agent (Met-L-X, graphite, copper). Never water — it can react
    violently and liberate hydrogen.
19. Class A: **75 ft**. Class B: **50 ft**.
20. The vapor is **heavier than air** and will travel along the floor and collect in low
    areas and pits, reaching remote ignition sources. Ventilation must be at floor level.
21. **Life safety → incident stabilization → property and environmental protection.**
22. When the employer has **10 or fewer employees**.
23. **Operations = defensive** — contain from a distance (dike, dam, absorb), never approach
    the point of release. **Technician = offensive** — approaches to plug, patch, or stop
    the release. Operations ≥8 hours training; technician ≥24 hours.
24. **Unity of command:** each individual reports to exactly one supervisor. **Unified
    command:** multiple agencies with jurisdiction jointly set objectives and share command
    of one incident.
25. Within **10 seconds / 55 ft** of the hazard on the same level with an unobstructed path;
    **15 minutes** of continuous flushing; **tepid, 60–100 °F**.
26. **PEL** (OSHA). TLV (ACGIH) and REL (NIOSH) are recommendations.
27. A **15-minute** TWA that must not be exceeded at any time during the workday. May occur
    **no more than 4 times per day**, with **at least 60 minutes** between exposures, and the
    8-hour TWA must still be met.
28. **4 µm** 50% cut point; deposits in the **gas-exchange (alveolar) region** of the lung.
29. Full-facepiece APR: **50**. Pressure-demand full-facepiece SCBA: **10,000**.
30. A change in the audiogram, relative to baseline, of an **average of 10 dB or more at
    2,000, 3,000, and 4,000 Hz** in either ear. Age correction is permitted.
31. **RCRA** regulates hazardous waste being generated and managed now — cradle to grave.
    **CERCLA** addresses cleanup and liability for past contamination at abandoned or
    uncontrolled sites.
32. **VSQG ≤ 100 kg/mo** · **SQG > 100 to < 1,000 kg/mo** · **LQG ≥ 1,000 kg/mo**.
33. Aqueous with **pH ≤ 2 or ≥ 12.5** (or corrodes steel faster than 6.35 mm/yr).
34. **Tier II: March 1.** **TRI Form R: July 1.** Both for the prior calendar year.
35. **Aspect** is the element of the organization's activity that *can interact with* the
    environment (the cause). **Impact** is the resulting *change* to the environment (the
    effect).
36. **Reaction · Learning · Behavior · Results.** (Phillips adds Level 5, ROI.)
37. **Level 1, Reaction.** It predicts almost nothing about behavior change or results.
38. One who is **capable of identifying existing and predictable hazards** in the
    surroundings or working conditions **and who has authorization to take prompt corrective
    measures** to eliminate them. Both halves required.
39. **Reactive → Dependent → Independent → Interdependent.**
40. A **slip** is a correct intention with a failed execution (an attention failure). A
    **mistake** is a flawed intention — the plan itself was wrong.
41. A hazard existed · the hazard was **recognized** · it was causing or likely to cause
    **death or serious physical harm** · a **feasible and useful means of abatement** existed.
42. Fatality: **8 hours.** Amputation, in-patient hospitalization, or loss of an eye:
    **24 hours.**
43. **15 working days** from receipt. If they don't contest, the citation becomes a **final
    order not subject to review**.
44. **Creating · exposing · correcting · controlling.**
45. Exposure records: **30 years.** Medical records: **duration of employment plus 30 years.**

# Scoring and what to do about it

Score each domain out of 5. Partial credit is fine — if you got the concept but missed a
number, count it as half.

| Domain score | Read |
|---|---|
| **5 / 5** | Solid. Review the "20 things they actually ask" list and move on |
| **4 / 5** | Minor gaps. Read the domain file once and drill the numbers |
| **3 / 5** | Real gap. Full read plus the practice questions for that domain |
| **≤ 2 / 5** | Priority domain. Schedule it early and revisit twice |

**Now weight it.** A 3/5 in Domain 2 (25% of the exam) costs you far more than a 2/5 in
Domain 9 (5%). Multiply your deficit by the blueprint weight to rank your study order:

```
Priority score = (5 − your score) × domain weight
```

| Domain | Weight | Your score | Deficit | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Math | 10 | | | |
| 2 Safety Programs | **25** | | | |
| 3 Ergonomics | 8 | | | |
| 4 Fire | 12 | | | |
| 5 Emergency | 10 | | | |
| 6 IH | 12 | | | |
| 7 Environmental | 7 | | | |
| 8 Training | 11 | | | |
| 9 Legal | 5 | | | |

Study in descending priority order. Take this assessment again at the two-thirds mark of
your study window — the delta tells you whether your method is working, not just your
seat time.

**One caveat on interpretation:** these are short-answer items, which are harder than the
exam's four-option multiple choice. A 60% here is not a 60% on the real thing. Use it to
rank domains against each other, not to predict a score.
