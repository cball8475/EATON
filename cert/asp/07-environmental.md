# Domain 7 — Environmental Management (7%)

~12 scored items. Small domain, mostly acronym recall and regulatory scope. Learn which law
covers which medium, and the RCRA generator categories cold — those are the reliable points.

---

## 1. The regulatory map — which law covers what

| Law | Medium / scope | Key mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **CAA** — Clean Air Act | Air emissions | NAAQS, Title V permits, NESHAP, NSPS, Title VI (ozone-depleting substances) |
| **CWA** — Clean Water Act | Surface water discharge | **NPDES** permits, pretreatment, stormwater, oil spill prevention |
| **SDWA** — Safe Drinking Water Act | Public drinking water and underground injection | MCLs, UIC program |
| **RCRA** | **Hazardous waste, cradle to grave**; also solid waste and **USTs** | Generator standards, manifest, TSDF permits |
| **CERCLA / Superfund** | **Abandoned/uncontrolled sites — past contamination** | NPL, PRP liability, remediation, release reporting (RQs) |
| **SARA Title III / EPCRA** | Community right-to-know and emergency planning | Sections 302, 304, 311/312, 313 |
| **TSCA** | New and existing **chemical substances** in commerce | PMN, inventory, PCBs, asbestos, lead paint |
| **FIFRA** | **Pesticides** | Registration, labeling, applicator certification, Worker Protection Standard |
| **NEPA** | **Federal actions** | Categorical exclusion → Environmental Assessment → Environmental Impact Statement |
| **OPA 90** | Oil spills to navigable waters | Facility response plans, liability |
| **DOT HMR (49 CFR)** | **Transportation** of hazardous materials | Classification, packaging, marking, labeling, placarding, shipping papers, training |

**The RCRA/CERCLA split is the most-asked distinction: RCRA governs waste being generated
and managed *now*; CERCLA cleans up contamination from the *past*.**

## 2. RCRA — hazardous waste

### Is it a hazardous waste?

First it must be a **solid waste** (discarded material — which can be liquid, solid,
semi-solid, or contained gas). Then it's hazardous if it's **listed** or exhibits a
**characteristic**.

**Listed wastes:**

| List | Source |
|---|---|
| **F** | **Non-specific source** — spent solvents, electroplating wastes |
| **K** | **Specific source** — industry-specific process wastes |
| **P** | **Acutely hazardous** discarded commercial chemical products |
| **U** | Toxic discarded commercial chemical products |

**Characteristic wastes — "ICRT":**

| Characteristic | Code | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **Ignitability** | **D001** | Liquid with flash point **< 140 °F (60 °C)**; ignitable compressed gas; oxidizer; ignitable solid |
| **Corrosivity** | **D002** | Aqueous, **pH ≤ 2 or ≥ 12.5**; or corrodes steel at >6.35 mm/yr |
| **Reactivity** | **D003** | Unstable, reacts violently with water, generates toxic gases, cyanide/sulfide bearing, explosive |
| **Toxicity** | **D004–D043** | Leachate from the **TCLP** test exceeds the regulatory level |

Note that RCRA ignitability (140 °F) uses a **different flash point** than NFPA 30's
flammable/combustible line (100 °F). That mismatch is a deliberate trap.

### Generator categories

| Category | Monthly generation | On-site accumulation limit | Accumulation time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VSQG** (very small quantity generator) | **≤ 100 kg/mo** (~ half a drum) | 1,000 kg | No limit while under 1,000 kg |
| **SQG** (small quantity generator) | **> 100 to < 1,000 kg/mo** | 6,000 kg | **180 days** (**270 days** if transporting >200 miles) |
| **LQG** (large quantity generator) | **≥ 1,000 kg/mo** | No limit | **90 days** |

**Acute hazardous waste (P-listed) thresholds are much lower: > 1 kg/month makes you an LQG.**

**Other numbers:** EPA ID number required for SQG and LQG · **manifest** (EPA Form 8700-22,
now e-Manifest) for off-site shipment · **biennial report** for LQGs · **Land Disposal
Restrictions (LDR)** notifications · **contingency plan** (LQG full plan; SQG basic
provisions) · personnel training (LQG: annual, documented; SQG: basic familiarity) ·
**satellite accumulation areas** at or near the point of generation, under the operator's
control, **up to 55 gallons** (or 1 quart of acutely hazardous), then 3 days to move it.

**Container management:** kept **closed except when adding or removing waste** (the single
most-cited RCRA violation), in good condition, compatible with the waste, labeled
**"Hazardous Waste"** with the accumulation start date and the hazards, **weekly
inspections**, aisle space, and secondary containment for tanks.

**Cradle-to-grave liability:** the **generator remains liable for the waste forever** — even
after a permitted transporter and a permitted TSDF take it. That's why generator audits of
disposal facilities matter, and it's a stock exam answer.

**Universal wastes** (streamlined rules): **batteries, pesticides, mercury-containing
equipment (including thermostats), lamps, and aerosol cans**. **Used oil** has its own
separate regulations — it is *not* automatically a hazardous waste.

**USTs** — Subtitle I: leak detection, spill/overfill prevention, corrosion protection,
financial responsibility, and closure requirements.

## 3. Clean Air Act

**Six criteria pollutants with NAAQS:** particulate matter (**PM₁₀ and PM₂.₅**), ozone (O₃),
carbon monoxide (CO), sulfur dioxide (SO₂), nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), and **lead (Pb)**.

Note **ozone is not emitted directly** — it forms in the atmosphere from NOₓ + VOCs in
sunlight. That's why VOC controls are ozone controls. Areas are designated **attainment**
or **nonattainment**; nonattainment areas carry stricter permitting (NSR, offsets, LAER)
while attainment areas use **PSD** with BACT.

- **Title V** — the operating permit program for **major sources** (generally ≥100 tpy of a
  criteria pollutant, ≥10 tpy of a single HAP, or ≥25 tpy of combined HAPs). A **synthetic
  minor** takes enforceable limits to stay under the threshold and avoid Title V.
- **NESHAP / MACT** — National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants, applying
  **Maximum Achievable Control Technology** to the **188 listed HAPs**.
- **NSPS** — New Source Performance Standards for new/modified sources by category.
- **Title VI** — stratospheric ozone protection. **Montreal Protocol** phaseouts of CFCs,
  halons, and HCFCs; technician certification, leak repair thresholds, and no venting of
  refrigerants. The **AIM Act** now phases down HFCs (which are greenhouse gases, not
  ozone-depleting).
- **Air pollution control equipment:** cyclones (large particles, cheap, low efficiency) ·
  **baghouses/fabric filters** (high efficiency on fine particulate) · **electrostatic
  precipitators** (fine particulate, high volume) · **wet scrubbers** (gases + particulate,
  generates a wastewater stream) · **thermal and catalytic oxidizers/RTOs** (VOC destruction)
  · **carbon adsorption** (VOC recovery at low concentration) · **condensers** ·
  **biofilters**.

**Note for the plant floor:** "RTO" in an air-permit context is a **regenerative thermal
oxidizer**. Don't confuse it with any site-specific use of the acronym.

## 4. Clean Water Act

- **NPDES permit** required for any point-source discharge to **waters of the United
  States**. Effluent limits are technology-based (BAT/BPT) plus water-quality-based where
  needed. **Discharge Monitoring Reports** are the compliance record.
- **Indirect dischargers** to a **POTW** (publicly owned treatment works) are governed by
  **pretreatment standards** and local sewer-use ordinances, not NPDES.
- **Industrial stormwater** — the **Multi-Sector General Permit (MSGP)** or a state
  equivalent, requiring a **SWPPP**, benchmark monitoring, and routine inspections.
  **Construction** sites disturbing **1 acre or more** (or less if part of a larger common
  plan) need a construction general permit and a SWPPP with **BMPs** — silt fence, inlet
  protection, stabilized construction entrance, sediment basins.
- **SPCC — 40 CFR 112**: required for facilities with aggregate **aboveground oil storage
  capacity over 1,320 gallons** (counting containers ≥55 gallons) or **completely buried
  capacity over 42,000 gallons**, that could reasonably discharge to navigable waters.
  Requires a written, **PE-certified** plan (Tier I/II qualified facilities may self-certify),
  **secondary containment** for the largest single container plus freeboard, inspections,
  training, and a 5-year review.
- **Oil sheen reporting** — a discharge causing a **visible sheen** on navigable waters must
  be reported to the **National Response Center (1-800-424-8802)** immediately.
- **Wastewater treatment sequence:** preliminary (screening, grit) → **primary**
  (sedimentation, physical) → **secondary** (**biological** — activated sludge, trickling
  filter) → tertiary/advanced (nutrient removal, filtration) → **disinfection** → sludge
  handling. **BOD, COD, TSS, pH, oil and grease, and nutrients** are the standard parameters.

## 5. EPCRA / SARA Title III

| Section | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **302** | Notify the **SERC/LEPC** if an **Extremely Hazardous Substance** is on site above its threshold planning quantity; designate a facility emergency coordinator |
| **304** | **Emergency release notification** — immediate report to the LEPC, SERC, and (for CERCLA hazardous substances) the **National Response Center** when a release exceeds the **reportable quantity (RQ)**, followed by a written follow-up |
| **311/312** | **Tier I/Tier II** chemical inventory reporting to the SERC, LEPC, and local fire department; **Tier II due March 1** each year for the prior calendar year. SDSs must be submitted or a list of chemicals provided |
| **313** | **Toxics Release Inventory (TRI) — Form R**, due **July 1** for the prior calendar year. Applies to facilities in covered NAICS codes with 10+ FTEs that manufacture/process >25,000 lb or otherwise use >10,000 lb of a listed chemical (far lower thresholds for PBTs like dioxin and lead) |

**The two dates — Tier II March 1, TRI July 1 — are directly asked.**

## 6. Environmental management systems — ISO 14001:2015

Same **Annex SL** structure as ISO 45001, so clauses 4–10 map identically:

4 Context · 5 Leadership · 6 Planning · 7 Support · 8 Operation · 9 Performance evaluation ·
10 Improvement. PDCA throughout.

**14001-specific concepts:**

- **Environmental aspect** = an element of an organization's activities, products, or
  services that **can interact with** the environment (e.g., solvent use).
- **Environmental impact** = the **change** to the environment resulting from an aspect
  (e.g., VOC emissions contributing to ozone formation).
- **Significant aspects** are determined by defined criteria and drive objectives, controls,
  and monitoring. Getting aspect vs. impact backwards is the classic 14001 question:
  **aspect is the cause, impact is the effect.**
- **Life cycle perspective** is required in the 2015 revision — consider upstream and
  downstream, not just the fence line.
- **Compliance obligations** replaces "legal and other requirements."

**Integrated management systems:** because 45001, 14001, and 9001 share the High-Level
Structure, organizations increasingly run one system with shared clauses (context,
leadership, documented information, internal audit, management review). That efficiency
argument is worth knowing for an essay-flavored multiple choice.

**Other frameworks:** **ISO 50001** (energy management), **ISO 14064** (GHG accounting),
**LEED** (green building), **GRI / SASB / TCFD** (sustainability reporting frameworks).

## 7. Waste and conservation hierarchy

**EPA's hierarchy, most to least preferred:**

1. **Source reduction and reuse** (pollution prevention — always the top answer)
2. **Recycling and composting**
3. **Energy recovery** (waste-to-energy)
4. **Treatment**
5. **Disposal** (landfill, incineration without recovery)

**Pollution Prevention Act of 1990** made source reduction national policy — a clean
parallel to the hierarchy of controls, and the exam draws the analogy: **eliminate the waste
at the source, don't manage it downstream.**

**Waste minimization techniques:** material substitution, process modification, improved
inventory control (**first in, first out** — expired chemicals are a major waste stream),
segregation (mixing a small hazardous stream with a large non-hazardous one makes the whole
volume hazardous — **never mix**), better housekeeping and containment, closed-loop
recycling, and reuse of solvents by distillation.

**Water and energy conservation:** metering and submetering first (you can't manage what you
don't measure), leak detection, process water reuse, cooling tower cycles of concentration,
high-efficiency motors and VFDs, lighting retrofit, compressed-air leak surveys (compressed
air is the most expensive utility per unit of work in most plants), heat recovery.

## 8. Environmental hazards on site

- **Spills and releases** — prevention (containment, transfer procedures, inspection),
  preparedness (spill kits sized to the credible release, trained responders, drainage
  control), and response (contain first, then stop the source if safe, then clean up,
  **then report**). Reporting is not optional: RQ releases to the NRC, sheens to the NRC,
  and state/local per the permit.
- **Storm drains** — know which drains go to the sanitary sewer and which go to storm.
  Marking and mapping storm drains is a basic SWPPP BMP, and "it went down the storm drain"
  turns a housekeeping problem into a reportable discharge.
- **Radon** — see Domain 6. EPA action level **4 pCi/L**.
- **Asbestos and lead in buildings** — **NESHAP** requires a survey before renovation or
  demolition, notification, and specific removal work practices. Lead: EPA **RRP rule** for
  target housing and child-occupied facilities; OSHA 1926.62 for the workers.
- **PCBs** — TSCA-regulated; legacy transformers, ballasts, and hydraulic fluid. ≥50 ppm is
  regulated.
- **Mold and indoor air quality** — moisture control; ASHRAE 62.1 for ventilation rates;
  CO₂ used as a **surrogate for ventilation adequacy**, not as a toxicant (>1,000 ppm
  suggests inadequate outdoor air).
- **Refrigerants** — Section 608 technician certification, no venting, leak repair thresholds
  by equipment type, and recordkeeping.

## 9. ESG and the EHS role

**Environmental, Social, and Governance** — the framework investors, customers, and
regulators use to evaluate non-financial performance.

| Pillar | EHS contribution |
|---|---|
| **Environmental** | GHG emissions, energy, water, waste and diversion rate, spills, permit compliance, biodiversity |
| **Social** | **Worker safety metrics (TRIR, DART, fatalities, SIF)**, health and well-being, labor practices, contractor and supply-chain safety, community impact, DEI |
| **Governance** | Board oversight of EHS risk, ethics, compliance management systems, transparency and assurance of reported data, risk management |

**Safety data is ESG data.** Its most common landing spot is the **S** pillar, and it is
increasingly **externally assured** — which raises the bar on data integrity, definitions,
and audit trails. That consequence (rate definitions and record accuracy now face external
audit) is the exam's angle on this.

**GHG accounting scopes (GHG Protocol):**

| Scope | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope 1** | **Direct** emissions from owned or controlled sources | Boilers, furnaces, company vehicles, process emissions, refrigerant leaks |
| **Scope 2** | **Indirect** emissions from **purchased energy** | Purchased electricity, steam, heating, cooling |
| **Scope 3** | All other **indirect** value-chain emissions | Purchased goods and services, business travel, employee commuting, transportation and distribution, use and end-of-life of sold products |

**Scope 3 is usually the largest and the hardest to measure.** Reported in **CO₂-equivalent**
using global warming potentials (CO₂ = 1, methane = ~28, N₂O = ~265, some refrigerants in
the thousands).

**Sustainability concepts:** the **triple bottom line** (people, planet, profit) · the
**circular economy** (design out waste, keep materials in use, regenerate systems) ·
**life-cycle assessment (LCA)** — cradle-to-grave or cradle-to-cradle quantification of
impacts · **greenwashing** — unsubstantiated environmental claims, a governance and legal
risk.
