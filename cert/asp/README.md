# ASP Study Guide (ASP11 Blueprint)

Built against the current BCSP **ASP11 Examination Blueprint, V.2024.04.24**. Every domain
weight below is the published weight, not an estimate.

## Exam facts

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Questions | 200 multiple choice, 4 options, one correct |
| Scored questions | ~176 (BCSP pilots ~12% unscored; you can't tell which) |
| Time | 5 hours max, computer-based at Pearson VUE |
| Calculator | **On-screen TI-30XS emulator only.** You cannot bring your own |
| Scratch | Whiteboard + dry-erase marker provided |
| Formula sheet | None. Formulas are embedded in the question stem — except when the question is testing whether you know the formula |
| Result | Pass/fail on screen at submit; score report through your Pearson VUE account |
| Retake | Minimum 6 weeks between attempts |
| Fee | $350 per exam sitting |
| Deadline | One year from application approval to sit |

### The calculator thing — read this

Your course says a TI-30XS is required for the math exercises. True for the class. On the
real exam the calculator is **on-screen software that emulates the TI-30XS**, and the test
center will not let you bring a physical one. Practice on a physical TI-30XS anyway — the
key layout and the order-of-operations behavior are identical, and muscle memory on
`log`, `2nd`, `x⁻¹`, and the `x10ⁿ` key is what saves you time. See
[`ti-30xs-drills.md`](ti-30xs-drills.md).

### The formula thing — read this too

"Formulas are embedded" is the single most misread fact about this exam. It does **not**
mean you can skip formulas. It means:

- You will be handed `RWL = LC × HM × VM × DM × AM × FM × CM` and still have to know what
  each multiplier is, which units it takes, and that `LC = 51 lb`.
- You will be handed the incidence-rate formula and still have to know that a needle-stick
  requiring only a bandage isn't recordable, so `N` is zero.
- Questions that test whether you know the formula come with **no** formula.

So: memorize the constants and the definitions, not necessarily the algebra. The
[`formula-sheet.md`](formula-sheet.md) marks which is which.

Two things fall out of that table.

**Domain 2 is a quarter of the exam.** It's also the domain that hides math (Ohm's law,
risk matrices, machine-guarding safety distance) and hides Domain 1 content (fall
distance, trenching slope, rigging). If you only had a week, you'd spend it here.

**Math is 10% as a domain but shows up everywhere.** Noise math is scored under Domain 1;
hearing conservation program requirements are scored under Domain 6. Fall-distance math is
Domain 1; the 6-ft trigger is Domain 2. Study them together, which is how this guide is
organized — [`01-math-and-physics.md`](01-math-and-physics.md) is the calculation engine,
and each domain file points back into it.

## How to use this

1. Take the [gap assessment](gap-assessment.md) cold. Don't study first — you need a real baseline.
2. Score it by domain. Anything under 70% is a priority domain.
3. Work the domain files. Every one ends with a **"20 things they actually ask"** list —
   those are the recall items, drill them.
4. Do math daily. 20 minutes, every day, on the physical TI-30XS. Math skill decays faster
   than recall does, and a math question is the only kind you can get right with certainty.
5. Take the [practice exam](practice-exam.md) at the 60% mark of your study window, timed,
   in one sitting, no notes. Then re-scope your plan against the results.
6. Last 48 hours: [`quick-reference.md`](quick-reference.md) only. No new material.

## What "passing" means

BCSP uses a criterion-referenced cut score set by a modified-Angoff panel, reported on a
scaled score. There is no published percentage. Working backward from candidate reports,
plan to hit **70%+ on clean practice material** to have comfortable margin. Do not aim for
"just passing" — the pilot items add noise you can't control.

## Honest scope note

This guide is built to the published blueprint and to standards as they read at the time of
writing. Two things you should verify yourself rather than trust any study guide on:

- **OSHA penalty dollar amounts** — adjusted for inflation every January. Learn the
  citation *categories*; don't memorize the figures.
- **Current standard editions** (NFPA 70E, ANSI Z10.0, ISO 45001) — the exam lags new
  editions, but confirm nothing has revised out from under you before test day.
