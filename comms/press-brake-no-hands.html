EHS Lessons Learned
Press Brake Hand Placement
Hands pinched between the ram and the part at the front face of the die
LL Number  Date Issued 
SiteSumter, SC Issued ByCharlie Ball, EHS
AreaSteel Fab and Copper Fab EquipmentAll press brakes
AudienceAll press brake operators, setup personnel, team leads, and supervisors — all shifts

What Happened

Three operators have been injured on Sumter press brakes since 2023. Three different machines, three different shifts, three different parts.

DateMachineEvent
8/29/2023PB14 Operator finished a bend and reached in to remove the part. His foot contacted the pedal, the upper die traveled down, and his hand was pinched between the part and the front of the die. Right ring finger fracture. Recordable, restricted work.
2/6/2025PB27 New operator was checking a bend angle during setup using a scrap piece of metal. The scrap bent and pulled his hand into the die. Partial amputation, right middle finger. Recordable, restricted work.
6/26/2026PB19 Operator's thumb was caught in the same pinch. Treated on site, first aid.

In all three events the hand was pinched between the ram and the part, at the front face of the die. That is not the mouth of the press. The machines were operating as designed.

Root Causes

  1. The guarding does not cover the point of injury. The laser scanner covers the mouth of the press, and only the middle of the gap. It does not cover the front face of the die, which is where the hand is when an operator holds a part, positions a part, or checks an angle.
  2. Parts were being held by hand. The common factor in all three events was a hand on the part inside the pinch point.
  3. Setup and part removal were not treated as hazardous. Two of the three injuries occurred outside a normal production cycle, while checking or retrieving.
  4. Hand tools were available and not in use. Tools were installed at the brakes as a corrective action after the 2023 and 2025 events and were present at PB19 on 6/26/2026.
  5. Use of the approved methods was never verified. Prior corrective actions were closed when the tools were installed, not on evidence that operators were using them.

Corrective Actions

  1. Hand tools (magnet, orange push bar, tongs) installed and maintained at every press brake.
  2. Press brake training program and method sheets updated, with annual review and operator sign-off.
  3. This lessons learned issued to all press brake operators and support staff on all shifts. Signed roster returned to EHS.
  4. Random verification audits by EHS and area supervisors, beginning on issue of this document.

Required Practice — All Press Brake Operators

No hands within 3 inches of the die. This applies during setup, during the bend, and when removing the part.

There are four approved methods for running a part without a hand in the pinch point:

  1. Magnet. Set the part on the magnet mounted on the back of the bottom die instead of holding it. Magnets do not hold copper, so this method is not available in the copper shop.
  2. Orange push bar. Feed, seat, and position the part with the bar.
  3. Tongs. Grip and hold the part. Best method for W-channel and U-channel parts.
  4. Bring the ram down onto the part first. Lower the ram until it holds the part against the die, take your hands off, then complete the bend. The press holds the part instead of your hand.

If a part cannot be run using one of these four methods, stop. Back the ram off, re-fixture the part, or get your supervisor before you run it.

Verification and Accountability

EHS and area supervisors will conduct random audits of press brake operations on all shifts to verify that one of the four approved methods is being used.

Running a part by hand, without one of the four approved methods, is a failure to follow procedure. It will be addressed on the spot, and consequences range from coaching and retraining up to being sent home for the day.

If a part or a situation makes the approved methods impractical, that is a problem to raise with your supervisor or EHS before you run it. It is not a reason to run it by hand.

Approved Hand Tools

Photo of the three approved hand tools Magnet, orange push bar, tongs

These tools are at every press brake. If any are missing from your machine, tell your supervisor before you run.

Supervisor Discussion Points

  • Which parts in your area are still being held by hand? Name them.
  • Are the magnet, push bar, and tongs present at every brake on your shift right now?
  • What does your crew do today when a part is too small or the wrong shape for all four methods?
Review this lessons learned with your crew and return the signed roster to EHS. Questions on a part that does not fit the four approved methods: contact Charlie Ball, EHS, before it runs.
