Press Brake Safety — All Shifts, Steel & Copper

Hands out of the pinch.
Every part. Every time.

Use a magnet, an orange push bar, or tongs — or no hands at all.

Why you're getting this

Three people have been hurt on our press brakes on three different machines. Same mechanism every time. Different operators, different shifts, different parts — the machine wasn't the problem.

WhenMachineWhat happened
8/29/2023PB14Reached in to remove a finished part, stepped on the pedal. Ring finger fractured.
2/6/2025PB27Checking a bend angle during setup with a scrap piece. The scrap bent and pulled the hand in. Partial amputation, middle finger.
6/26/2026PB19Thumb caught in the same pinch. Treated on site. Two inches over and it's the same story again.

Where it actually gets you

It is not the mouth of the press. It's the front of the die.

Every one of these injuries happened at the pinch between the ram and the part, at the front face of the die — not inside the mouth. The laser scanner guards the mouth, and it only covers the middle of the gap. The place your hand goes is the place the laser doesn't watch. Don't let a guarded machine talk you into a free hand.

The rule

No hands within 3 inches of the die — front, side, or mouth.
Not to hold a part. Not to nudge it square. Not to check an angle. Setup counts. A scrap piece in your hand is still your hand. "Just this once" is how all three of these started.

Your three tools

Photo of the three approved tools Magnet · orange push bar · tongs — left to right, matching 1 · 2 · 3 below
1
Magnet
Mounted on the backside of the bottom die. Set the small part on the magnet instead of holding it.
Will not hold copper. In the copper shop, use a push bar or tongs.
2
Orange Push Bar
One on every brake. Feed, seat, or nudge the part into position with the bar — not your fingers.
3
Tongs
Grip, hold, and retrieve. Best option for W-channel and U-channel parts.
If the job won't run with one of these three — stop.

Back the ram off, re-fixture it, or get your supervisor. Do not freehand it and do not work around it to save a cycle. The part is replaceable. Your hand is not.
